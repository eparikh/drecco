<p> You are given a <strong> n </strong> by <strong> n </strong> chessboard. You and your opponent have <strong> b </strong> bishops and <strong> k </strong>
knights at your disposal. </p>
<p> The game is played in turns. On your turn, you can move one of your pieces or place a remaining piece on the board. </p>
<p> A state is considered winning if there are no <strong> legal </strong> moves left for the opponent. </p>
<p> Note that this definition causes stalemates to be impossible. </p>
<p> A move is <strong> legal </strong> if none of your opponent's pieces can directly capture your king after the move. If you have no legal moves, you lose. </p>
<p> Your goal is to leave your opponent with no legal moves. </p>
<p> <strong> Rules: </strong> </p>
<p> - <strong> Bishops </strong> can move diagonally in any direction. </p>
<p> - <strong> Knights </strong> can move in an L-shape (two squares in one horizontal or vertical direction, then one square in the other direction). </p>
<p> - The <strong> King </strong> can move in any direction, but only one square at a time. </p>
<p> - A new piece can be placed on any empty square that does not directly threaten the opposing king. </p>
<p> - After the end of your turn, there must not be any pieces that can directly capture your king. </p>
<p> - If a player has no legal moves left, then the opposing player wins. </p>
<p> - After <strong> m </strong> turns, the game is a win for black in recognition of the first-move advantage that white enjoys. </p>

<p> Note that when setting the time, you can enter any non-numerical input to disable the time limit (for example, "X"). </p>
<p> Some suggested game-sizes: </p>
<p> - n = 5, b = 4, k = 4 for a small game, making maneuvering pieces an added challenge. </p>
<p> - n = 8, b = 5, k = 5 for a more standard game. </p>
<p> - n = 10, b = 10, k = 10 for a large game with many pieces. </p>
